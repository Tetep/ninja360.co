Game Plan

Where We Are

The goal for today: leave with a first topic, a guest list, and a recording date. Everything else can wait. We aimed to record in September; mid-November is now the earliest.

What's ready: the studio is wired with six cameras, the shirts were due Monday, the microphone lower third is picked, and 19 people have signed up as guests. What's missing: confirmed guests, a camera layout that works for a full panel, and a sponsor for episode one. The ESPN producer's note on our test footage: the wide shots are too wide and the close-ups too tight. Gen Z is the gap: 2 of the 19 signups.

The Plan, One Through Seven

Seven steps take us from the signup list to a published show. Steps 1 to 5 run in order; steps 6 and 7 run alongside them.

  1. Fix the signup survey (this week). Same link. The responses sheet is done: its Topic Matrix tab gives every topic its own column and updates itself. Still to do is the form: one checkbox per category, with example topics as text underneath.
  2. Pick the topics and panels (Wed Nov 4). Two topics both Gen Z signups can cover (numbers below). Four guests per topic, one from each generation. Erik invites each with two date options.
  3. Lock the studio (Wed Nov 4). Test the V table layout. Fix the framing: pull the wide shots in and loosen the close-ups. Mark where a sponsor sits for one question.
  4. Record two episodes in one day (week of Nov 9). Thu Nov 12 afternoon or the weekend of Nov 14–15. Weekends will likely suit Gen X, Y and Z guests. If Nothing Bundt Cakes signs on, the panel eats their cake during one question, not in a separate ad.
  5. Edit and build a runway. The full episode takes about 7 days after taping, the clips about 10. We publish only once two episodes are done, then every other week.
  6. Close the Gen Z gap (starts now). Every promo this month asks for Gen Z first: Erik's Gala on Saturday, the Northland Chamber giveaway, and Erik's school district contacts.
  7. Clean up the social accounts (by end of November). Instagram and TikTok move to info@generationallyincorrect.com (Tim). YouTube and Facebook get checked and switched (Erik). X: keep it or drop it. One shared login under info@ owns each account; Tim and Erik are each admin under their own profiles, with Google Authenticator on both phones. Then decide on social media help: a revenue-share pro, a student intern, or doing it ourselves.
The gate

The Dec 1 launch holds only if step 4 happens by Nov 15. Later than that, the launch moves.

Getting Seen

YouTube is home base. Everything else points people there, and every post asks one of two things: watch the full episode, or be on the show.

  1. One home. Full episodes go on @GenerationallyIncorrect. Each title is the episode's question.
  2. Clips do the work. Gabe cuts 6–10 clips per episode. One goes out each day, Tuesday to Saturday, and the same clip goes to Shorts, Reels, TikTok and Facebook.
  3. Local first, in person. We start in rooms we're already in:
    • Erik's ACA Gala, Sat Nov 7: 70–80 business leaders. Plug the show and point people to the signup link.
    • The Northland Chamber giveaway (Erik and Gabe).
    • Smithville Rise and Shine and the Northland chambers.
    • Restaurant lines on Friday and Saturday nights: ask people waiting one question on camera. The best answers become clips and signups (Tim).
    • The school districts Erik is already vetted with (North Kansas City, Smithville, Liberty): an intern, and Gen Z guests.
  4. Sponsors live inside the show. Food during one question, or a question matched to the sponsor. No ad break for anyone to skip.
  5. One scoreboard, checked Mondays. Signups (Gen Z counted separately), subscribers, watch hours, clips posted. YouTube starts paying at 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in a year, the bar Gabe pulled up on Monday's call.

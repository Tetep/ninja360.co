04 · How a stop runs

Four beats, about a minute each

Beat 1

The ask

“Got a minute for a quick debate?” Mic out, camera rolling.

Beat 2

Pick your generation

They name their generation on camera. Instant label for the clip.

Beat 3

The question

The question of the week. Then one push-back: “What would a Boomer say?”

Beat 4

The scan

“Think you can defend your generation? Scan to be on the show.”

Everyone on camera agrees to appear before the clip is used. [Release method: card, QR checkbox or sign: TBD]

05 · From scan to seat

A passerby becomes a guest

Scan

QR on the mic flag

Every scan is tagged with the venue and the date.

GHL first

Fan list

They land in GHL: on the fan list, tracked by venue.

Invite

Apply

Cast

A seat at the table

Screened, pre-interviewed, voted on by all three of us, then booked.

Bonus: we learn which venues produce the most fans and the most guests.

07 · Testing topics

The street decides what gets a taping

Ask

Test 2-3 questions

Rotate a few candidate questions across the crowd at each stop.

Score

Who argued back?

Rate each one: did people disagree, get louder, call a friend over?

Pick

Winners go to the table

The best scorer goes to the topic vote. The weak ones get dropped cheaply.

A topic that splits a restaurant line will split the table.

08 · Good for sponsors

The venue is the first sponsor

What they get

“On the Road at [Venue]”

Their name in the segment title, their sign in the shot, a tag in every clip from that night.

What we give them

A recap package

Clips they can post, plus how many people we talked to and how many scanned.

What it grows into

Paid local spots

Proof from the street becomes the pitch for an episode or season sponsorship.

Rule: every venue or sponsor deal runs through the show’s LLC, not anyone personally.

11 · Templates & agreed strategy

Locked before the first outing

  1. Question card. The question of the week plus one push-back line, same layout every week.
  2. Run of show. The four beats, word for word, so any host sounds like the show.
  3. Shorts template. Title format, captions, black, red and white end card pointing to the channel.
  4. Consent card. The quick on-camera release, checked by the attorney.
  5. QR & venue tags. One naming rule for codes and GoHighLevel tags so every venue is tracked the same way.
  6. Venue recap & one-sheet. What we hand the venue after the night, and what we pitch sponsors.
